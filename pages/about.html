§ About

A short note.

On the path from Sinonin to Nakuru, the farmers I work with, and what I'm trying to do in the next few years.

I'm Charity Jepchirchir. I grew up in Kenya and went through school at Sinonin Primary, then St. John's Sigowet Secondary. In 2018 I started a degree in Agriculture and Biotechnology at Masinde Muliro University of Science and Technology, graduating in 2022 with Second Class Honours, Upper Division. I'm now based in Nakuru, working full-time in agricultural extension and seed systems.

The interest in farming started early. It was the work around me, in the soil, in the seed, in the weather. Studying agriculture and biotechnology gave a name and a method to what I was already curious about. The shape of what I do now sits at the intersection of three things: soil health (because what's underground decides what comes out of it), seed systems (because the farmer who plants poor seed loses the season), and farmer extension (because the best research is the kind that ends up in someone's plot).

The work

I've been with Farm Inputs Promotions Africa (FIPS-Africa) since November 2023, first as a Network Coordinator and, since April 1, 2026, as a Multi-Network Coordinator after a promotion. The role is still hands-on, just at a wider scope. Training smallholder farmers in sustainable agronomic practices, soil health, and climate-smart technologies. Coordinating farmer groups across several networks and helping them access improved inputs. Setting up and monitoring learning demonstration plots in their communities. Running plant health clinics where farmers walk in with a sick crop and walk out with a diagnosis and a plan.

A growing share of the work is on potato seed systems. We're supporting seed production and distribution, and trying to push more clean, certified seed into the smallholder supply. That's where the KEPHIS Private Seed Inspector certification comes in. I sat the national exam in 2025 and am now in the official training, sponsored by FIPS-Africa.

Before FIPS, I volunteered with the MMUST university farm for most of 2023, working on crop establishment, management, harvesting, soil sampling, and fertility testing. Earlier still, in 2021, I interned at Mabanga Agricultural Training Centre in Bungoma, helping run farmer training on soil fertility and crop management.

What I'm aiming at

I want to keep working at the intersection of research and practice, particularly in soil health, seed potato, and climate-smart farming. The longer goal is to contribute to Kenya's food security agenda and, in time, to position my work as part of a broader push for sustainable agriculture and climate adaptation across Africa. There's still a lot of distance between what's in the journals and what's in the field. I'd like to spend my career helping close that gap.

Outside of work

I read on extension methods and plant health, and I sit in on workshops whenever there's a useful one near me. I speak English and Kiswahili fluently, and I'm a careful, practiced field communicator, which matters more than it sounds when you're explaining a soil pH test to someone who's farmed the same hill for thirty years.
